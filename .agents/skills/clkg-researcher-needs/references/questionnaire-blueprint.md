# CLKG questionnaire blueprint

## Use and format

Ask respondents to answer for one concrete cultural heritage research task from the past two years. Target 12–15 minutes. Use a consent gate, 60% closed questions, about 25% five-point scales, and two or three open questions. Include `Not applicable` or `Not sure` on all matrices. Do not mix heritage type and data modality in one item.

## Core instrument

| Section | Questions | Primary response format | What it measures |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Consent | Q0 voluntary participation | single choice | ethical validity |
| Context | Q1 role; Q2 research stage; Q3 heritage scope; Q4 intended outputs; Q5 one-sentence research problem | multiple choice, multi-select, short text | task and sample context |
| Materials and workflow | Q6 material/data modalities; Q7 sources; Q8 need for cross-source linkage; Q9 actual steps; Q10 rework example; Q11 current tools | multi-select, short text | data journey and current practice |
| Friction | Q12 difficulty matrix; Q13 top-three priorities; Q14 recurring data problems | 5-point scale, rank, multi-select | shared and differentiated pain points |
| Evidence and governance | Q15 importance matrix; Q16 uncertainty handling; Q17 required human approval; Q18 rights and risks | 5-point scale, multi-select | provenance, review, and governance needs |
| Outcome and ownership | Q19 valuable capabilities; Q20 acceptable-data conditions; Q21 responsibility allocation; Q22 shortcomings plus case; Q23 optional follow-up contact | ranked multi-select, multi-select, short text | value, quality threshold, governance, recruitment |

## Q3: heritage scope

Ask: “Which cultural heritage types does this research task involve? Select all that apply.”

### A. World cultural heritage and immovable heritage

- Monument and architectural heritage
- Groups of buildings, historic towns, or historic environments
- Archaeological sites and archaeological places
- Cultural sites
- Designed cultural landscapes
- Organically evolved cultural landscapes
- Associative cultural landscapes
- Cultural routes or linear heritage

### B. Movable cultural property and collections

- Archaeological objects, antiquities, coins, seals, inscriptions, or excavated collections
- Artistic, religious, or historical objects
- Ethnographic, folk, or community-life objects
- Scientific, technological, industrial, or social-history collections
- Museum, specimen, or thematic collections
- Other movable cultural property

### C. Documentary heritage and Memory of the World-related materials

- Manuscripts, rare books, newspapers, or publications
- Archives, letters, ledgers, registers, or institutional records
- Historic maps, drawings, photographs, or printed images
- Sound, film, video, or other audiovisual records
- Born-digital or digitised documentary collections
- Other documentary heritage

### D. Intangible cultural heritage and living heritage

- Oral traditions and expressions, including language
- Performing arts
- Social practices, rituals, and festive events
- Knowledge and practices concerning nature and the universe
- Traditional craftsmanship
- Other living-heritage practice

### E. Cross-type scope

- This task combines two or more types above
- This task centres on relations among people, places, objects, events, and documents and cannot be represented by one type
- Other

Keep all selections available. UNESCO regimes overlap and should not be represented as a single exclusive taxonomy in the instrument.

## Q12: difficulty matrix

Use: 1 Almost no difficulty; 2 Minor difficulty; 3 Moderate difficulty; 4 Major difficulty; 5 Extreme difficulty; Not applicable.

- Locating original materials
- Cleaning, transcribing, or converting formats
- Recognising the same object across different sources
- Handling place names, locations, coordinates, or spatial extent
- Handling dates, periods, or vague temporal expressions
- Keeping sources linked to research statements
- Handling contradictory or uncertain information
- Collaboration, version control, and review
- Turning materials into citable research outputs

## Q15: importance matrix

Use: 1 Not important at all; 2 Slightly important; 3 Moderately important; 4 Important; 5 Extremely important; Not applicable.

- Original source and reference number
- Who created, entered, or modified information and when
- Processing, conversion, and interpretive steps
- Temporal and spatial precision or uncertainty
- Alternative interpretations and conflict records
- Copyright, consent, and access conditions
- Method for maps, statistics, networks, or other derived outputs

## Q22: shortcomings plus concrete case

Ask: “What are the main shortcomings of your current way of organising materials? Select up to three.”

- Materials are dispersed across personal devices, paper records, cloud drives, or institutions
- No stable identifiers exist for people, places, events, or heritage objects
- Names, times, places, or classifications differ across sources
- Original materials, structured records, and final claims cannot be traced together
- Uncertainty, contradiction, or multiple interpretations cannot be recorded well
- Spatial or temporal information is difficult to standardise
- Collaboration, version history, and review are unclear
- Text, image, audio, tables, GIS, and other modalities cannot be linked easily
- Copyright, consent, sensitive-location, or access controls are unclear
- Derived maps, statistics, and network results cannot be reproduced
- Data are hard to share, cite, migrate, or reuse
- Current practice is broadly adequate
- Other

Then ask: “Please describe one concrete case in which the main problem occurred and its effect on the research task.” Keep this optional and suggest 100–200 words.

## Conditional modules

Show modules only when Q6 selected their material type.

- GIS: position source, spatial precision/confidence, raw-versus-derived layers, and reproducibility of spatial analysis.
- Oral history/community: consent, access scope, original-recording/transcript links, attribution, and participant/community confirmation.
- Documentary heritage: original/copy/transcription relations, catalogue identity, version/date/source ambiguity, and document-to-entity links.

## Pilot checks

Ask 6–8 participants to think aloud while answering. Record terms they misunderstand, questions they skip, answer options they cannot use, and the time required. Revise before the formal survey; do not calculate or report reliability from a tiny cognitive-pilot sample.
