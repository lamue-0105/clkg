# From researcher-needs evidence to CLKG claims

## Evidence coding

Code each finding as one of the following and do not collapse them:

- **Observed fact**: a workflow, source, artefact, or tool the participant showed or described concretely.
- **Expert judgment**: a domain interpretation, quality rule, or acceptance criterion requiring further triangulation.
- **Future wish**: an expected benefit or requested capability; treat as a design hypothesis.
- **Constraint**: a legal, ethical, community, institutional, or technical boundary.

Assign evidence strength: `single self-report`, `concrete task example`, `multiple participants`, `artefact-supported`, or `requires external validation`.

## Required synthesis outputs

For every proposed CLKG requirement, write:

1. **Finding:** what participants did, needed, or could not do.
2. **Evidence:** respondent/task context, evidence strength, and supporting quote or artefact reference.
3. **CLKG implication:** relevant concept, metadata, workflow, or governance constraint.
4. **Boundary:** what human judgment or external rule remains outside automation.
5. **Next test:** interview, card sort, sample audit, prototype evaluation, or policy confirmation needed before acceptance.

## Analysis cautions

- Report whole-sample results separately from heritage-type, role, and modality subgroup results.
- Treat multi-select heritage categories as overlapping labels; do not sum them as if they were exclusive groups.
- Use counts and percentages for closed questions, median or distribution for ordinal scales, and thematic coding for open responses.
- Preserve disconfirming cases. A subgroup that reports no need for a feature is evidence about scope, not noise.
- Keep raw responses, coded data, codebook, and aggregated results as separate versions with access restrictions.
