# Visual Standards for History and Social Science Figures

## Palette

Use semantic roles rather than arbitrary decoration:

| Role | Default color | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Primary structure | `#244A63` | title, core question, main spine, key borders |
| Evidence/material | `#EAF4F7` with `#8FB9C8` border | archives, data, interviews, maps |
| Theory/literature | `#F1F5FA` with `#A9BBD2` border | debates, concepts, theoretical lenses |
| Method/analysis | `#EDF7F3` with `#A8CDBE` border | methods, networks, GIS, coding |
| Contribution/output | `#FFF7E8` with `#E4C37A` border | findings, contribution, policy, heritage |
| Uncertainty | `#F5F3F8` with dashed `#9E94B8` border | tentative, inferred, or pending relations |

Use dark text `#314A5A` and light secondary text `#6A7F8C`. Do not rely on color alone: use shape, labels, or dashed lines to distinguish semantic classes.

## Typography

Prefer a CJK-capable sans-serif stack such as `Noto Sans CJK SC, Source Han Sans SC, PingFang SC, Microsoft YaHei, Arial, sans-serif`. Use one family throughout unless a journal requires otherwise.

For a 1200–1600 px working canvas:

- figure title: 26–34 px, semibold;
- section or group title: 18–24 px, semibold;
- node heading: 15–19 px, semibold;
- node body: 12–16 px;
- edge labels: 11–13 px;
- minimum text: do not go below 11 px in the working SVG.

For final journal placement, test the figure at the actual printed width. Text must remain readable after reduction; do not solve crowding by shrinking labels indefinitely.

## Lines and Arrows

- primary arrows: 1.8–2.4 px;
- secondary or organizational connectors: 1.2–1.6 px;
- tentative or inferred relations: dashed 5 4;
- comparison or feedback: double-headed or curved only when semantically necessary;
- use arrowheads consistently and keep them outside node borders;
- do not let connectors pass through text or unrelated nodes.

## Layout

Use 8–12% outer margins. Align boxes to a grid. Keep equal semantic levels at a common baseline. Use whitespace between levels instead of heavy borders. A figure should normally have one dominant reading path and no more than two crossing edges.

For literature reviews, use a 2×2 or horizontal four-strand arrangement followed by one central synthesis and one bottom gap/contribution band. For research frameworks, use 4–6 horizontal stages or a macro–meso–micro vertical hierarchy. For network/GIS studies, separate data construction, network analysis, spatial interpretation, and historical explanation rather than mixing them in one dense node.

## Caption Pattern

Use a caption that states what the figure explains, not merely what it contains:

> 图X 研究框架。研究以【核心问题】为主线，结合【理论/视角】与【材料/数据】，通过【主要方法】分析【机制/历史过程】，进而解释【核心发现或学术贡献】。
